# Code of Conduct

The Tayku Web Runtime (TWR) project adopts this Code of Conduct to foster an open, welcoming, safe, and respectful community for everyone. As an open-source project rooted in technical clarity, modular simplicity, and structural discipline, we commit to making participation in our community a harassment-free experience for all contributors, developers, maintainers, and community members.

This document serves as the official community contract for human interaction across all TWR channels and development environments. It aligns directly with the behavioral standards established by major open-source ecosystems such as the Linux Kernel, ensuring that technical excellence, architectural integrity, and mutual respect remain the pillars of our development ecosystem.

---

# 1. Purpose

The purpose of this Code of Conduct is to establish explicit behavioral standards for TWR community interactions, define unacceptable conduct, outline enforcement responsibilities, and guarantee a civil, professional environment across all official venues of the Tayku Web Runtime project.

# 3. Definitions

This section defines terms used normatively throughout this community specification.

- **Participant:** Any human contributor, developer, maintainer, or user interacting within TWR project spaces.
- **Maintainer:** An authorized individual or core team member responsible for reviewing contributions, managing repositories, and enforcing project guidelines.
- **Project Space:** Any physical or digital venue directly managed, owned, or officially recognized by the TWR project.
- **Harassment:** Unwelcome, hostile, offensive, or coercive personal behavior directed at individuals or groups.

---

# 4. Scope

- **in-scope:**
  * Official TWR source code repositories, issue trackers, pull requests, and code review discussions.
  * Official mailing lists, forums, IRC channels, and community chat platforms dedicated to TWR.
  * Documentation files, commit messages, and inline code comments.
  * Public representation of TWR at technical conferences, workshops, or official project events.

- **out-of-scope:**
  * Private personal communication channels unrelated to the TWR project.
  * Third-party repositories, tools, or external projects not governed by or affiliated with the mainline TWR project.

---

# 5. Our Standards

To foster a positive, productive, and professional environment, participants in the TWR community MUST adhere to the following behavioral standards:

- **Demonstrate Respect and Dignity:** Participants MUST treat every contributor, maintainer, user, and community member with professional courtesy and respect regardless of background, level of experience, or personal characteristics.
- **Use Professional Language:** Participants MUST use clear, professional, and non-discriminatory language in all written and verbal technical communications.
- **Focus on Technical Merit:** Discussions SHOULD prioritize code quality, contractual correctness, performance, and ABI stability over personal preferences.
- **Show Professional Courtesy:** Senior developers and maintainers SHOULD mentor and guide newer contributors patiently, promoting an environment of transparent knowledge sharing.

---

# 6. Unacceptable Behavior

The following behaviors ARE STRICTLY PROHIBITED within any TWR project space:

- **Personal Attacks and Insults:** Participants MUST NOT direct abusive, derogatory, or insulting language toward individuals in technical discussions, issue trackers, or code reviews.
- **Harassment:** Personal attacks, political attacks, unwelcome sexual attention or advances, and deliberate intimidation or trolling MUST NOT be tolerated.
- **Doxxing and Privacy Violations:** Participants MUST NOT publish private personal information—such as physical or electronic addresses—without explicit permission from the affected individual.
- **Disruptive Conduct:** Participants MUST NOT engage in sustained disruption of technical discussions, spamming, or deliberate derailment of issue trackers and code reviews.
- **Unprofessional Conduct:** Any conduct that is reasonably considered inappropriate in a professional software engineering environment MUST NOT be permitted.

---

# 7. Technical Communication and Critical Disagreement

TWR strictly distinguishes between direct technical critique and hostile or abusive personal behavior:

- **Legitimate Technical Critique:** Strong, direct, and rigorous technical disagreement is an essential part of open-source engineering. Statements critiquing technical implementation, architectural validity, memory safety, POSIX compliance, ABI contracts, or the author's technical understanding (e.g., *"You do not understand the ABI requirements of this interface; this design therefore violates the ABI contract"*) ARE ACCEPTABLE.
- **Prohibition of Personal Attacks:** Technical critiques MUST NOT become personal attacks, harassment, or abuse. Criticism MUST remain relevant to the technical matter under discussion and MUST NOT use insults, humiliation, threats, or derogatory language against the author (e.g., *"You're an incompetent idiot"* is strictly prohibited).
- **Constructive Code Reviews:** Code review feedback SHOULD be direct, concise, actionable, and professionally expressed. Direct or negative technical conclusions are permitted and MUST NOT be treated as personal attacks solely because they are critical or strongly worded.

---

# 8. Enforcement Responsibilities

Project maintainers ARE RESPONSIBLE FOR:

- **Clarifying Standards:** Maintaining clear documentation regarding community expectations and behavioral guidelines.
- **Fair Investigation:** Investigating reported violations promptly, impartially, and confidentially.
- **Content Moderation:** Maintainers HAVE THE RIGHT AND RESPONSIBILITY to reject contributions, request required changes, remove inappropriate comments, close issues, or revert changes that violate this Code of Conduct. Maintainers DO NOT have the authority to arbitrarily edit the commit history or source code of contributors without authorization.

---

# 9. Enforcement Guidelines

When a violation of this Code of Conduct occurs, maintainers MAY apply the following enforcement measures based on the severity and frequency of the infraction:

· · ·

## 9.1 Correction

- **Community Impact:** Minor or unintentional use of inappropriate language or unprofessional behavior.
- **Consequence:** A private, written warning from project maintainers, providing clarity on the nature of the violation and explaining why the behavior was inappropriate. An apology MAY be requested.

· · ·

## 9.2 Warning

- **Community Impact:** A direct violation through a single incident or a series of disruptive actions.
- **Consequence:** A formal warning with explicit consequences for continued unacceptable behavior.

· · ·

## 9.3 Temporary Suspension

- **Community Impact:** A serious or repeated violation of community standards, including sustained hostile behavior.
- **Consequence:** A temporary ban from participating in TWR communication channels, repositories, or official spaces for a specified period.

· · ·

## 9.4 Permanent Ban

- **Community Impact:** A persistent pattern of severe violations, including sustained harassment, doxxing, or deliberate sabotage of the project community.
- **Consequence:** A permanent ban from all TWR repositories, communication channels, and community spaces.

# 10. Questions

## 10.1 Why Does TWR Adopt Linux-Style Community Rules?

Adopting proven Unix/Linux community guidelines ensures that TWR remains focused on technical excellence, architectural stability, and civil collaboration while preventing toxic or counterproductive behavior.

· · ·

## 10.2 Who Enforces These Rules?

These rules are enforced by the maintainers of the mainline TWR project as documented in `tayku-ai-memory-bank.md`.

· · ·

## 10.3 How Do I Report an Incident?

Instances of abusive, harassing, or unacceptable behavior MAY be reported directly to the project maintainers. All complaints MUST be reviewed and investigated promptly and fairly, and maintainers MUST respect the privacy and security of the reporter.
